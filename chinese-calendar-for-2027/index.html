Understanding the Chinese Calendar: A Blend of Lunar Cycles and Zodiac Signs

Unlike the Gregorian calendar most of us use daily, the Chinese calendar is lunisolar. This means it tracks both the moon phases and the solar year, causing its months and festivals to shift yearly.

Each year pairs one of the 12 zodiac animals with one of the five elements—Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, or Water—resulting in a 60-year cycle that repeats. Be sure to explore march calendar as well. 2027’s combo is the Fire Rabbit, an intriguing mix that colors the year’s energy.

The Lunar Months and Leap Months

Every lunar month starts with a new moon and ends with a full moon, roughly 29.5 days each. To keep the calendar aligned with the solar year, a leap month is inserted every two or three years. 2027’s calendar incorporates this adjustment to keep festivals on track.
